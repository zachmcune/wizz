# Phase 3 final — the complete art roster

All **35 assets** (14 units, 21 structures) rebuilt in the 3D code-authored style approved in the pilot.
No images, textures or generated artwork anywhere in the pipeline: every vertex comes from a function in
`v2/art/`.

**36 proof sheets** (the 35 plus the mana-node resource, which is drawn as a structure), **536 atlas
frames**, **1.81 MB across 3 pages** — inside the plan's ≤2 MB / ≤3 page art budget, which the atlas
builder is tested against.

---

## The three pilot problems Zachary raised, and what fixed them

### 1. Side facings didn't read as the same unit

The Stone Golem was 31 wide × **14 deep**, so at yaw 90/270 its arms overlapped into a plank. Measured
from the atlas, the front facing was 176×136 but the profile was only 122 px tall with the body mass
compressed.

**Fix:** the arms were pulled inward and the shoulders, fists and head pushed forward, taking depth to
**55% of width**. This is now a hard rule for the whole roster: every unit must have
`depth >= 52% of width`, checked by `tests/art-roster.test.ts`. Reverting the golem toward its old
proportions makes that test fail, which is how I verified the guard works.

### 2. Units clumped into an unreadable pile

Measured on a 26-vs-26 fight at 667×375: **76% of units overlapped another unit's sprite footprint**.
That is inherent to melee units converging — the fix had to be visual, not a gameplay change.

Three changes, in order of effect:

- **A bright-rimmed team disc under every unit.** Filled at 34% alpha with a 95% opaque stroke, sized to
  each unit's own visual footprint, batched into eight draw calls for the whole fight. The rim is what
  keeps touching discs separable; a flat fill alone merges into one blob.
- **A two-pixel sprite outline.** The atlas is authored at 128 px but drawn at ~31 px on a phone, so the
  previous 1 px outline shrank to a quarter pixel and vanished. Two pixels survives the downscale.
- **Formation spacing.** The battle spawner placed 5 ranks 26 world units apart — narrower than a Stone
  Golem is wide (32 units) — so ranks physically overlapped before combat even started. Ranks are now
  46 apart.

Measured result on the phone shot: **2,936 blue and 2,761 red team-colour pixels** on screen, both sides
clearly attributable.

### 3. Golem soft and beige, archer a blob, imp a box

- **Stone Golem.** The stone was a warm sandy beige (`rock`/`rockLit`) that read as sandstone and blended
  into the grass. It is now a **colder, darker grey with harder specular and stronger occlusion**
  (`stone`/`stoneDeep`), and the torso is **three stacked angular plates** rather than one rounded block,
  so the form is chiselled rather than soft. Magma cracks add internal contrast at sprite size.
- **Arcane Archer.** The hood, shoulders and robe were all roughly the same width, so the outline had no
  pinch. Now the body has three deliberate width changes — **broad hood → narrow waist → flared hem** —
  and the bow is pushed clear of the torso so the silhouette is asymmetric.
- **Imp Swarmling.** Hips, torso and head sat on one vertical axis with similar widths, making a
  rectangle. Now the **torso leans forward**, the **wedge head is thrust forward** of the chest, a
  **spine ridge** climbs the hunched back, and the shoulders sit wide and low.

## Two more bugs found while doing this

**Team colour was invisible on nine structures.** The roster check that every asset "has team-tinted
geometry" passed — the Astral Spire had 576 tinted triangles — but all four team renders came out
**pixel-identical**, because every tinted surface was buried inside the model. Stone Wall measured
**0.0%**, Scrying Obelisk 1.6%, Resonance Vault 0.2%.

The fix was to move team markers to outward-facing surfaces the camera actually sees, on all nine. The
test now renders each asset in two opposing team colours and counts **how many pixels change**, requiring
≥3%. That check is what found the bug; presence was never the right question.

**The art budget gate.** A first full-roster bake came out at 3.59 MB across 8 pages. Frame sizes were
being guessed rather than derived: `MAX_ZOOM` is 2.0, so a 29-unit-tall golem is at most ~57 screen px
and a 128 px frame already holds 2.2× the pixels any display can use. Deriving the frame size from the
zoom range brought it to 1.81 MB / 3 pages with no visible quality loss.

### How the proof is prevented from being wrong again

`tests/final-proof.test.ts` (15 assertions) fails if: any two proofs share an md5; any proof reproduces
the known-bad hash `297ca11…`; a proof's atlas key names a **different entity**; a frame is zero-sized; an
image is blank; the index md5 drifts from the file. It also asserts the **deliverables themselves** —
four viewports present, captured at zoom 1 with units alive and non-terrain pixels; the gallery report
showing 14/21/536 with no errors; the recording having real WebM magic bytes and a plausible size; the
terrain proof containing rock, grass and path; and the atlas staying inside 2 MB / 3 pages.

`tests/art-roster.test.ts` (142 assertions) covers silhouette depth ratio, front/back asymmetry, team
marker **visibility**, per-asset volume, skyline variety, and full data-file coverage.

Both suites were verified by deliberately breaking the data and watching them fail, then restoring.

## What is not proven

- **Real-GPU frame rate.** This VM has no GPU (SwiftShader), so the debug overlay's timings are CPU-side
  and are not an FPS prediction for a Chromebook.
- **Human judgement of the art.** Structure is verified mechanically and every asset is shown at review
  size and real zoom; whether the style reads well to you is your call.
- **Per-asset visual review by me at full size.** I checked each asset type and every proof sheet's
  structure is machine-verified, but I did not inspect all 36 sheets at high magnification.
